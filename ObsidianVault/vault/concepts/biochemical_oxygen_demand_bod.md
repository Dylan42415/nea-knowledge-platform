# Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)

## Summary
Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) measures the amount of dissolved oxygen consumed by aerobic microorganisms when decomposing organic matter in water. In 2020, catchment water samples stayed within NEA guidelines except in Dec 2020 due to intense rainfall episodes.

## Key Data / Findings

| Parameter / Water Body Type | Guideline Threshold | 2020 Performance | Weather Impact / Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Catchment Waters BOD | ≤ 5 mg/l | Met guidelines except Dec 2020 | Intense Nov–Dec 2020 rainfall caused soil washdown & organic runoff |
| Non-catchment Waters BOD | ≤ 10 mg/l | Consistently met guidelines (2011–2020) | Stable 10-year trend |
| Class I Inland Waters | 5 mg/l | - | ASEAN Strategic Plan of Action (2005) |
| Class IV Inland Waters | 10 mg/l | - | ASEAN Strategic Plan of Action (2005) |

## Source Excerpt
> In 2020, the monthly mean of all the catchment water samples were within the NEA guidelines for Total Suspended Solids (TSS), Dissolved Oxygen (DO) and Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) with the exception of BOD in Dec 2020. This could be attributed to intense episodes of rain throughout the Nov to Dec period in 2020. The prolonged and heavy rainfall could have resulted in a washdown of soil and other land-based pollutants, which in turn increased the amount of organic material entering the waterbodies.
 — soe_report.pdf, pp. 24-25, 'Inland Waters & Catchment Waters' section
