# Emerging Contaminants (ECs)


## Summary
Emerging contaminants (ECs) are synthetic or naturally derived water pollutants receiving increased scrutiny due to eco-toxicological risks and potential bioaccumulation in urban aquatic ecosystems. They occur at low concentrations (ng/L to μg/L) in urban watersheds and enter the urban water cycle primarily via wastewater treatment plant discharges, sewer leakage, and runoff.

## Key Data / Findings

| Category | Representative Compound Classes | Typical Occurrence Levels |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical Classes | Persistent organic pollutants, hormones, EDCs, PPCPs, PFASs, disinfection by-products, nanoparticles | ng/L to μg/L in urban surface waters |
| Biological Classes | Antibiotic resistant bacteria (e.g., ESBL E. coli), antibiotic resistance genes | Variable (e.g., 10 CFU/100mL, 10⁻⁵–10⁻² relative abundance) |
| Marker Applications | Artificial sweeteners (acesulfame, cyclamate, saccharin) | Sewage leakage & diffuse source markers |
