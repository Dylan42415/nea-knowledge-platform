# Enterococcus


## Summary
Enterococcus is a bacteriological indicator used to measure faecal contamination and assess public health risks in water bodies. Coastal waters exceeded the 200 counts/100ml threshold in May 2020, but all seven monitored recreational beaches maintained a 'Good' status overall for 2020.

## Key Data / Findings

| Monitoring Site | Guideline Threshold | 2020 Performance | 10-Year Annual Trend (2011–2020) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coastal Waters | ≤ 200 counts/100 ml | Exceeded guideline in May 2020 | Annual mean levels remained relatively stable |
| Recreational Beaches | ≤ 200 counts/100 ml (95th percentile) | All 7 beaches achieved "Good" banding | Slight increasing trend over 10 years, with recent 3-year improvements |

## Source Excerpt
> Enterococcus is a type of bacteria normally found in the faeces of humans and warm-blooded animals. It is widely used to indicate the level of faecal contamination in water. Faecal contamination in water can cause negative health impacts such as gastrointestinal and respiratory infections.
