# Dissolved Oxygen (DO)

## Summary
Dissolved Oxygen (DO) represents the concentration of oxygen gas dissolved in water. Catchment DO levels in Singapore declined from 2015 to 2017 due to El Niño weather (hotter conditions and lower rainfall), with steady improvements over the subsequent 3 years.

## Key Data / Findings

| Parameter / Water Body Type | Guideline Threshold | 2020 Performance | Weather Impact / Historical Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Catchment Waters DO | ≥ 5 mg/l | Met monthly mean guidelines | Declined 2015–2017 (El Niño heat/drought), improved over last 3 years |
| Non-catchment Waters DO | ≥ 3 mg/l | Maintained above guideline (2011–2020) | Stable 10-year trend |
| Coastal Waters DO | ≥ 4 mg/l | Met guideline for all months of 2020 | Monitored across 43 points & 8 buoy stations |
| Class I Inland Waters | 5 mg/l | - | ASEAN Strategic Plan of Action (2005) |
| Class IV Inland Waters | 3 mg/l | - | ASEAN Strategic Plan of Action (2005) |

> Dissolved Oxygen (DO) levels in catchment waters however showed a decline from 2015 to 2017 with improvements observed subsequently in the recent 3 years. The decline in DO performance coincided with the El Nino weather, where hotter conditions and lesser rainfall were experienced. At higher water temperatures, waterbodies tend to hold less dissolved oxygen.
